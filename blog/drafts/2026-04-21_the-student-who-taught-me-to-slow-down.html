Lead Paragraph (≈170 words)

There is a particular discomfort that good teachers learn to sit with: the student is struggling, the answer is obvious, and your hands want to move. You want to write it on the board, type it into the chat, say the word that would end their confusion in three seconds. In Korean there is a phrase—*기다려*, wait—that sounds simple but requires everything. I have been teaching long enough to know that the lessons I remember most are the ones where someone waited for me. Not because they didn't know, but because they understood that knowing and arriving are two different things. AI changes this calculus in ways we have barely begun to examine. A system trained to minimize wrong answers will, by design, minimize the space between question and resolution. That space, it turns out, is where most of the learning lives. This issue is about what we lose when we optimize for speed—and what Jeju's quieter rhythms might teach us to protect.
