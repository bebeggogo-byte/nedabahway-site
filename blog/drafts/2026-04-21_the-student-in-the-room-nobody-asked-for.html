Lead Paragraph (~170 words)

Last month, I sat across from a room of fourteen social workers in a community welfare center near Jeju City. They had been told — not asked — to attend a smart-work training session. Their manager had signed the contract. Their schedules had been cleared. Their presence was, in the polite language of institutional life, *arranged*. I have stood in front of rooms like this many times. There is a particular quality to the silence before you begin — not the silence of anticipation, but of people who have already decided this will not be useful to them. They are not wrong to feel that way. Most training is built by people who will never sit through it, for people who were never consulted about it. AI has made this faster and cheaper to produce, but it has not made it more honest. This is an essay about what it means to design education *for* someone versus *with* someone — and why the preposition matters more than the curriculum.
