Lead Paragraph

Every lecture I give, there is a moment I have learned to love: the silence after the hardest question. Thirty adults staring at the floor, or the ceiling, or somewhere just past me. By every productivity metric, this is waste — dead air, idle minds, a trainer failing to fill the hour. I used to rush through it. Now I let it run.

We are building tools that are extraordinarily good at removing pauses. Slides generated in seconds. Summaries distilled before anyone has sat with the confusion. In Jeju, the haenyeo divers have a saying that doesn't translate cleanly, but the shape of it is this: *the sea gives nothing to the diver who is already thinking about the surface.* Presence before ascent. The still point is not the delay — it is the work.

This issue is about what we stand to lose when we optimize learning the way we optimize logistics. Not a rejection of AI. A question about which frictions are load-bearing.
