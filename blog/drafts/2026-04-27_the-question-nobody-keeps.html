Lead Paragraph (~175 words)

There is a moment, just before a workshop begins, when the room is still full of questions. Not the questions on the slide deck — those are already answered, more or less — but the real ones, the ones participants brought in their pockets: *Does this apply to me? Is my way of working already obsolete? Am I still useful?* A good facilitator knows this moment. A bad one rushes past it, eager to fill the silence with content. I've been running education sessions across Jeju for several years now, often in community welfare centers and rural training halls. What I've noticed is this: the rooms that change people are not the rooms with the best material. They are the rooms where someone — a trainer, a colleague, sometimes a stranger at a side table — was willing to sit with a question long enough for others to realize they had the same one. That's not a technique. It's a kind of generosity that no AI system has been asked to replicate. Yet.
