Lead Paragraph (~175 words)

There is a particular silence that falls over a classroom when a student already has the answer — not because they studied, but because they asked a machine. I have stood inside that silence. For a moment you wonder if you are still needed, and then, if you are honest, you wonder if you ever asked yourself that question before the machines arrived to ask it for you. Teaching in Jeju, I notice that the island itself moves at a pace most curricula cannot afford. The haenyeo do not rush the sea. The tangerine orchards do not accelerate for a quarterly target. And yet, for years, I taught as though content delivery were the whole of education — pressing forward, filling minutes, staying ahead of the room. AI broke that habit the way a storm breaks a bad roof: suddenly, and usefully. What stands now is the structure worth keeping: the part where I do not know something, say so, and learn alongside the person in front of me.
